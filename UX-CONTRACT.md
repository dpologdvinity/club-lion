# Club Lion interaction contract

The user requested a Club Penguin-like website using lions. The first complete slice is a local, single-player game with an explorable savanna, scripted neighbors, character styling, a free shop, and an arcade of seven activities: Memory Safari, Bee Stop, Paw Steps, Fruit Catch, DJ Beat Drop, Smoothie Kitchen, and Savanna Screamer. Downtown Plaza, Wonder Park Entrance, Carnival Midway, and Club Pulse extend the neighborhood. [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md) owns visual decisions. [src/game.ts](src/game.ts) owns progression rules. [src/beeStop.ts](src/beeStop.ts) owns Bee Stop scoring.

## Flows and invariants

- Clicking the world or using arrow/WASD keys while its button is focused moves the lion within the foreground. Movement never captures keys in chat or modal inputs.
- Map choices, destination previews, and My den navigate to the same place and record the visit. Navigation is immediate and reversible.
- Neighbors are scripted game characters. Greeting the same lion twice cannot inflate adventure progress. Chat and emotes stay on the current screen and are not transmitted or stored.
- Style edits use a preview draft. Save requires a nonblank name of at most 16 characters and applies the chosen color and owned accessory. Closing the dialog discards the draft. This is a small, reversible customization flow, so cancellation does not need a confirmation.
- There is no currency. A new lion starts with the forest scarf. Every Shop item is free: collecting one adds it to the player once, enforces ownership, and immediately equips the accessory or places the item in the den. Saves written when coins existed still load and ignore the old balance.
- Each of the three starter adventures can be claimed once. The neighborhood adventure requires 3 distinct greetings; the game adventure requires a completed game; the home adventure requires a den visit. There are no daily resets in this slice.
- Memory Safari has 6 shuffled pairs, no time limit, and a move counter. Card pairs lock while resolving. Finishing counts one played game. Closing an incomplete game counts nothing; replay starts a fresh board. Pending timers are canceled on unmount.
- Bee Stop is 10 rounds with no fail state, so every round is always scored and every completed run counts as a played game. The bee sweeps faster and its scoring bands narrow each round, and the flower spot is a fixed sequence so skill carries between runs instead of luck deciding a round. Bands are perfect 100, good 60, okay 30, miss 0. The best score only ever rises. The flower bar is a single button that accepts a pointer, Space, or Enter, and ignores held-key repeats so one press scores one round. The bee position is written to a transform during the frame loop, and the animation frame is canceled on unmount.
- Under reduced motion the bee hops between a fixed number of visible steps per second instead of gliding. Scoring and round length are identical, and the petal bloom records one petal per round rather than only perfect stops, so a run without perfects still shows progress.
- Paw Steps shows a sequence of paw arrows, starting at 2 and growing by one each round, then asks the player to repeat it with the arrow keys or the pad buttons. Wrong taps lock input, reveal the arrow that was expected, and end the game. Because the sequence grows forever, ending a game is the only completion: it counts one played game and keeps the highest round count in `pawStepsBest`. Leaving mid-round counts nothing.
- Paw Steps ignores held-key repeats and advances accepted input synchronously. A stable game live region announces each sequence, including the opening and replay, and the expected direction after a mistake. Focus moves to the pad for playback and input, and to Play again when the run ends.
- Fruit Catch is a 30-second keyboard and touch game. Catching ripe fruit scores 10 points; catching rotten fruit costs one of three hearts. Its best score persists, while older saves default it to zero.
- Bubble Pop opens straight into relaxed play: big bubbles float up over the savanna or the beach with no score, timer, or way to lose. A tap, click, Enter, or Space pops one with a procedural pop, a sparkle burst, and sometimes a surprise (lion cub, mango, star, bunny, chick, or fish) that joins a small tray. Focus moves to the next bubble after a keyboard pop. A trophy toggle switches to a 60-second challenge: same-color pops chain a combo worth up to five times the points, and rare golden bubbles extend any chain. A finished challenge counts once through `completeBubblePop` as a played game and keeps `bubblePopBest`; leaving early or switching back to relaxed play counts nothing. The clock only counts while the page is visible. Under reduced motion, bubbles rest in a still grid instead of floating, and bursts fade instead of flying.
- Coloring Book shows six line-art pages as picture cards. Every fillable part of a page is its own SVG region, reachable by tap or by Tab and Enter, and named for screen readers. Round swatches of at least 56px pick 16 colors, a rainbow, a sparkle fill, or the eraser; undo steps back through the current visit, and the wand fills the page from a random five-color palette. The check button marks a page finished; the first finished page awards the First Masterpiece stamp once, with no coins. Each change saves to the additive `coloringBook` field. Restoring drops unknown pages, regions, and colors, and older saves load without the field.
- The arcade menu lists all games by name with each game’s best score, and hands results to `src/game.ts`. Room triggers launch DJ Beat Drop, Smoothie Kitchen, Savanna Screamer, or Fruit Catch directly; the Games header opens the full menu. closing the dialog discards the choice along with any unfinished run. All games offer a return to menu action, which refocuses the selected game card and pays nothing for unfinished runs.
- Non-sensitive progress is saved in versioned localStorage. Invalid saves recover to a fresh player. `beeStopBest` and `pawStepsBest` are additive fields, so saves written before these games existed restore those fields as 0 instead of being rejected, and tampered values are capped at the maximum. Storage failures show a persistent warning while leaving the game usable. Other browser tabs receive saved changes through the storage event; simultaneous edits follow browser last-write-wins behavior.
- Environmental lighting (Day, Sunset, Dusk, Night) is global: `GlobalLightingOverlay` renders over every room, panoramic or raster, without intercepting controls, the avatar, or portals. Automatic mode follows the local clock and resyncs every minute; manual selection disables automatic mode. Preferences persist in localStorage; an invalid or missing saved time recovers to the current clock time rather than crashing, and automatic mode initializes from the live clock instead of a stale saved value. Storage failures leave lighting usable in-session and surface the same persistent save warning as player progress. All star and firefly motion freezes under reduced motion, including when the preference changes mid-session.
- Panoramic rooms share `src/rooms/registry.ts`, declarative manifests, and `CameraViewport`. The camera follows and clamps in logical stage units; its viewport-sized ground converts pointer positions with one camera offset and the stage scale. Arrow/WASD movement clamps to the walkable polygon. Each portal carries a safe arrival spawn and latches while occupied, preventing immediate bounce or repeated catalog openings. Named path/activity buttons provide keyboard alternatives to off-camera targets.
- World stage coordinates locate the avatar’s feet. Club Pulse’s dance floor uses the same bounds for its drawn tiles and footfall lookup; tile presses also move the avatar to that tile center. Sound defaults off; direct tile presses can activate procedural audio when sound is enabled. Reduced motion freezes ambient ride scenery and dance-floor pulses, and suppresses board sparkles while preserving gameplay.
- DJ Beat Drop completions use `completeDJBeatDrop` to derive payouts from score/combo, count a completed game, and save the best. Smoothie serving uses `completeSmoothieOrder` to guard payouts and increment served orders. Unfinished activity exits pay nothing. Both direct and arcade launches share the App Games `Dialog`; Smoothie Kitchen’s standalone wrapper remains for fixtures, and its reusable body creates no nested modal.
- Savanna Screamer is a scenic ride with no played-game reward. The ride’s completion photo is available for local viewing/download only. Ferris wheel, flume, teacups, and carousel are spectator scenery; their named controls describe the spectacle without claiming a playable ride. The midway game booth launches Fruit Catch.
- All destinations derive from `PLACES`; added visits survive V1/V2 restoration under the existing save key. Current room and position stay local to the open session and reset to Savanna Square on reload unless an active world restores its saved room. Scene vectors are rendered directly; illustrative manifest asset names never cause missing image requests.
- Pet Paradise Nursery is reachable from Downtown Plaza and the map. Its named grooming hotspot and room activity button open the shared native dialog. Wash, Brush, Treat, and Play update the persisted pet stats immediately; reaching 100% happiness awards Pampered Pride once in the same state update, with no coin or played-game reward. Closing the dialog keeps completed care. Older V1/V2 saves default missing care stats to 60; saved stats are validated and bounded to 0–100 without discarding other progress. Fullness rises when fed. Sound defaults off and uses procedural synthesis through the shared SFX bus when enabled. Decorative effects respect reduced motion and their timer is canceled on exit.
- Local-only, non-shareable room and dialog state remains in React. No account, network request, retention policy, billing workflow, or private data is required.

- Friends stores only IDs in the local save (100 friends, 100 requests in each direction, 15 recent visitors). The panel accepts a session profile directory for online status, current rooms, and username lookup; missing profiles are offline and cannot receive jumps or emotes. The local app has no connected player directory and does not present scripted neighbors as online friends. Graph edits persist; live request delivery and presence belong to the caller supplying profiles and callbacks. Jumps use safe walkable positions outside portal triggers, including jumps within the current room.

- Servers opens the shared native dialog with keyboard-accessible Browse Worlds and Host New Lounge tabs. The three official worlds and custom lounges have local occupant counts. Joining or hosting moves to the selected room, leaves the previous membership, and resets the scene; rejected joins preserve the current membership. Capacity includes the host (default 25, bounded to 1–50). Hosts can mute, unmute, kick, or ban other occupants; guests have no moderation controls. A kick permits rejoining, while a ban blocks it. These actions update local lounge state; no live player transport is connected. Optional passwords are stored as local join gates in the browser, with no remote authentication claim. The active world, its room, and moderation state are validated and restored under `club-lion-active-server-v1`; other lounges remain available for the current session only. Leaving clears the active save. Invalid saves recover to the official list, and storage failures show a warning while keeping the session playable. This separate save leaves the player save format unchanged.

## Accessibility and verification

English interface, native semantics, keyboard movement, named controls, field associations, visible focus, accessible modal behavior, reduced motion, and responsive document flow are baseline requirements. Browser tests exercise desktop and mobile, success and validation paths, lost storage, rewards, persistence, all seven arcade activities, lighting preference recovery and global rendering, and automated WCAG AA scans.

Verification uses Playwright Chromium against the Vite server, with temporary screenshots and reports outside source control.

- Toast messages are also read aloud with the browser's speech engine, emoji removed, so pre-readers hear what happened. Speech follows the master volume and stays silent while the master or sound-effects channel is muted.
- Savanna Square is the starting room and a manifest room (1536 × 864 stage over the painted `savanna.webp`, top sky cropped). Its toys react in steps: the fountain splashes, gushes and shows a rainbow (the rainbow hums a three-note tune); the big tree shakes, shows a monkey and drops mangoes; three bushes hide a bunny, a hedgehog and a bird; lanterns glow, flags gain a parrot, the sandbox grows a sandcastle and the swing swings. Six flowers each play a marimba note, and any music sets the neighbors dancing for 3 seconds. Tapping your doll makes her twirl; the player card opens from the ID Card button.
- The square's picture doors go to the café, Le Shop, games, your den, Downtown Plaza, Wonder Park and Pet Paradise, saying the place name first.
- The weather toy cycles sunny, rain, rainbow, snow. It is not saved.
- Five butterflies hide in the square each local calendar day. Each tap counts once (`findSquareButterfly`), progress is saved as `squareButterflies` and validated on load, a new day starts a fresh hunt, and the fifth find earns the Butterfly Catcher stamp once with a confetti burst. Every toy in the square answers with a sound and, where it has words, reads them aloud (the flowers answer with their note); nothing has a fail state, stops moving under reduced motion, and tap targets stay at least 48 screen px at any camera zoom.
- Mango Run is in the Games list and the arcade ("Run for mangoes"). The lion runs down an endless trail grabbing mangoes; bumping a rock is a silly bounce with a boing, never a lost life. The child taps All done! to end the run, which counts one played game and keeps `mangoRunBest` through `completeMangoRun`; closing mid-run counts nothing.
- Lola's Kainan is a Filipino restaurant off Downtown Plaza (bottom-row portal, 2400 × 720 stage). Lola stands by the counter and greets with "Kain tayo! Let's eat!". Toys: the rice cooker steams and opens, the sinigang pot stirs and bubbles, the lechon turns, the sari-sari shelf pops out a snack per tap, the fan spins slow then fast, the jeepney honks and fills with animal riders, and the karaoke hums a tune that sets everyone dancing. The halo-halo machine opens Lola's Halo-Halo.
- Dino Dig is a desert fossil site off Downtown Plaza (bottom-row portal, 2400 × 720). Four sandy spots dig in two taps to a skull, a leg bone, a shell fossil and a giant tooth; the tap that uncovers the last one unlocks the Fossil Finder stamp (session dig progress, stamp saved). The volcano puffs, then erupts with a falling tune that sets everyone dancing; the dino egg wobbles, cracks and hatches; the sifter shakes out a gem. Pip waits by the door, Cleo mid-site.
- Pin of the Day (Club Penguin style): each local day one of 14 collectible pins hides high in a room (`pinForDay`, every pin before any repeats). The "Pin hint board" in Downtown Plaza names today's room. Tapping the twinkling pin keeps it in `pins` (once; validated on load) with a "N of 14" toast; 5 different pins earn the Pin Collector stamp.
- Lola's Halo-Halo (Games list and the Kainan machine): tap up to 6 treats into a tall glass, take one off, then serve. Copying Lola's picture order exactly is 3 stars, the right treats in any order 2, any other cup 1; "Make my own" free play is always 3. Every served cup says "Masarap! Salamat!", counts one game and `haloHaloServed` through `completeHaloHalo`, and unlocks the Halo-Halo Chef stamp.
- Carry-anywhere props (Toca Boca style): fifteen rooms (Kainan, watering hole, café, arcade, den, plaza, beach, Snowflake Rink, Wonder Park Entrance, Coastal Pier, Lion Aquarium, Mt. Mist, Canyon Rapids, Lion Speedway, Pet Paradise; the busy square has none so its open grass stays tappable) have toys and snacks from `manifest.props`. Drag one anywhere on the stage; a tap makes it hop. Drop it within 110 stage px of a neighbor to give it: the neighbor says "<emoji> Yum yum! Thank you!" (food) or "<emoji> For me? Thank you!", it is read aloud, everyone dances, and a fresh prop returns to its starting spot after 1.6 s. Keyboard: Enter picks a prop up, arrow keys carry it, Enter drops it. Put-down spots are saved (`propSpots`, keyed `room:prop`, clamped to the stage on load); a given-away prop goes home. Drop a prop within 110 stage px of your doll's body (100 px above her feet) and she holds it: it rides beside her hand as she walks, and tapping it (a 48 px button, "Put down the …") sets it down at her feet. Holding is session-only and one prop at a time. Props start above the bottom room bar so taps reach them on desktop.
- The arcade is a 6000-wide hall with a cabinet for every game (25, `arcade-<gameId>`), each showing its game's picture on the screen and opening that game; the prize machine and disco ball sit in the middle, Milo waits by the entrance (phones arrive there), Pip mid-hall (the desktop map lands there), and the carry props sit near the door, the leaderboard is at the left wall and "Choose a game" at the far end.
- Pop the Fireflies (Games list, an arcade cabinet and "Catch fireflies" at the watering hole): a 30-second night. Fireflies blink; a tap while one glows (or within 250 ms after) catches it, golden ones are worth 3, and a tap on a dark one just wiggles it. The round ends on its own, counts one game, keeps `fireflyBest` through `completeFireflies`, and a jar of 15+ earns the Firefly Catcher stamp. Reduced motion stops the drifting but keeps the blinking.
- Peekaboo Animals (Games list, an arcade cabinet and "Play peekaboo" in Pet Paradise): 8 rounds. Six animals hide in bushes and peek out one by one (900 ms each, 75 ms quicker every round, never under 350 ms), then a picture calls one to find. A wrong bush only shows who is there; finding it on the first try fills the round's star. The game counts once at the end, keeps `peekabooBest` through `completePeekaboo`, and 6+ first-try finds earn the Peekaboo Pro stamp.
- Dress-the-Pet (Games list, an arcade cabinet and "Dress up a pet" in Pet Paradise): pick any of the 8 pet species, read the theme card (beach, party, royal ball, snow day) and put on a hat, eyewear, neckwear and an extra. "Show time!" scores every look: 1 star, 2 for 2–3 theme matches, 3 for all four. Each show counts once through `completeDressPet` (`petShows`), and a 3-star look earns the Pet Stylist stamp. The next theme is never the same twice in a row.
- Pet Round-Up (Games list, an arcade cabinet and "Round up pets" in Pet Paradise): a 60-second round on a grassy field with a pen in the top-right corner. Nine pets amble about; pressing near a pet shoos it away from the finger (holding and sliding herds them like a sheepdog), dragging a pet carries it, and a plain tap or Enter on a pet hops it toward the pen. Penned pets stay inside unless shooed out again, so there is no fail state. The round ends at 60 s or as soon as all nine are in; the score is the pets penned, plus the seconds left when all are in. It counts one game, keeps `petRoundUpBest` through `completePetRoundUp`, and penning every pet earns the Pet Wrangler stamp. Reduced motion stops the wandering and smoothing; pets move only when shooed, tapped or dragged.
